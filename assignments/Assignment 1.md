# Assignment 1: Create a dataset

{: .due }
**Due: 9 am, Fri Oct 16 → Submit to [Canvas](https://canvas.mit.edu/courses/40342).**

Mimi Onuoha’s art project “[The Library of Missing Datasets](https://github.com/MimiOnuoha/missing-datasets)” illuminates what we value and what we ignore in an increasingly data-saturated world. For example, there is little data that exists about financial transactions in the art market (especially between gallerists and artists) and on US judges and case outcomes. _Think of a dataset that doesn’t exist and create it._

This could be as grandiose or as mundane as you would like: it could be the temperature of a patch of grass outside your room for a week, FitBit or other health tracking data, or more substantial research on social or political phenomena. (For example, the [Data Against Feminicide](https://datoscontrafeminicidio.net/en/home/) project required scores of volunteers to track feminicide throughout Latin American by reading through innumerable news articles to collect data that governments did not.) There is no requirement around time span or number of observations in this dataset; the “[Dogs of Cambridge](https://data.cambridgema.gov/General-Government/Dogs-of-Cambridge/sckh-3xyx/data)” dataset is not bounded by time the way that temperature is. This dataset _does_ need to demonstrate clear inclusion and exclusion criteria, require some kind of measurement apparatus, and provide you with enough information to do some analysis about what the data shows. To complete the assignment, you will submit the dataset, a write-up about the data collection process / what the data shows, and a data visualization of your choice.

## Data collection protocol
1) Data set: What is your data set about? What gap is it filling? 
2) Unit of observation: what is one row of data? What does each number or observation represent? What kind of data is it (e.g., continuous, categorical, etc)? 
  a) Tidy data (Wickham 2014): each variable is a column, each observation is a row, and each observational unit is a table 
3) Inclusion criteria: what belongs in the dataset? 
4) Exclusion criteria: what would be excluded from the dataset? 
5) Measuring instrument: how are you collecting the data? With what? 
6) Scope: how do you decide when a dataset starts and ends? What is the schedule or duration of your dataset? 
7) Missing data: how are you dealing with missing, blank, null, etc. data? What does a blank, NA, or 0 mean in the context of your data? 
8) Storage: file format? File name convention? 
9) Claims: what kinds of arguments could you make with this data?
